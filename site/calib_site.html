Noise and limits

The repeatability σ comes from a repeat study: many dips at one level, without pouring. Your study at 501 mm and 1033 mm gave 0.2–0.4 mm. Every ± here means "the truth is almost surely inside" (2σ).

Timers, alarm and device

Fill (pump) time per can, m:ss

The time the pump needs to fill one can. The alarm rings this time minus the lead, so the valve is closed when the can is full. The timer needs this number: a tap gap cannot give it, because the gap also holds the carry and the drain.

How the check works

The question. You just read the tape. Is the reading good, or must you dip again? You must know now: after the next pour that level is gone.

Test 1: the prediction. The page knows your diameter and length estimates, the readings you accepted, and the pour. From these it learns the tank shape, and it predicts the level that this pour must give. If your reading falls inside the band, accept it and pour the next can.

Test 2: the repeat reading. If the reading misses the band, or if no prediction is possible (the first level, an unknown pour), the page asks for a second reading. It hides the first reading and the prediction, so that the second reading cannot copy them. Two readings that agree within about 1 mm make a good dip.

The first pours. The page does not know the bottom shape yet. For the first 3 intervals a confirmed miss is "learning": the level is kept and teaches the shape.

Too wide to judge. When the band is wider than ±3 mm (very large pours, an early shell), the prediction cannot catch a 3 mm slip. Then the page asks for a second reading.

What it catches. In a simulation of 100 campaigns per slip size, a reading off by 3 mm was caught 92 % of the time, and 4 mm or more every time. An honest reading triggered a second dip 3–6 % of the time.

Drain campaign (reverse strapping). The tank starts full. A pump draws the water out into calibrated cans; each batch is one step, and the level falls. Start at the neck entrance: pump the neck down until the level just reaches the shell crown and dip. That reading is the brim, and the running shell knows the top from the first level. Keep the hose in the same state at every dip. Fill each can to its mark. When the pump loses suction, tick pump lost suction: last can part.

Signs in a drain. resid < 0: the fall came too large (an object exposed, a leak, a spill at the can, or more left than the count). resid > 0: the fall came too small (RETURN?: the hose drained back, wall film, or less left than the count).

Two tanks at once (transfer). A pump draws water out of a full tank A into calibrated cans, and the same cans are emptied into tank B. One batch is one step for both tanks: A is a drain, B is a fill. Each tank keeps its own check, its own running shell and its own raw file; switch tanks in the header or in Log & charts. Dip A, then dip B, after each batch — the dip card has one field and one button per tank, so each tank is checked as soon as its reading is in. When both dips are in, the page compares the two residuals of the same batch: the same litres in both tanks means the cans (a count or fill error) — one recount fixes both files; litres that only B misses means a spill on the way; a miss that only one tank shows means that tank or its hose. The pour noise is shared, so the comparison is as sharp as the dips alone.

Method: CALIBRATION_METHODOLOGY.md §10a. Residual convention §2: resid = pour − predicted pour. resid < 0: the rise came too cheap (object, or more liquid than recorded). resid > 0: the rise came too small (loss, or less liquid than recorded).
